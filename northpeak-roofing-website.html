Frequently Asked Questions · Homeowner Guide

Answers to Common Roofing Questions

Practical information regarding residential and commercial roofing considerations in Ontario’s climate.

Key indicators that a roof is nearing replacement include curled, cracked, or missing shingles, excessive dark granules collecting in eavestroughs, visible sagging along the ridge, or interior water stains on ceilings. If your roof is between 18 to 25 years old and experiencing widespread issues, full replacement is generally more cost-effective than recurrent partial repairs.
Roofing project investment is primarily determined by total square footage, the steepness (pitch) of the roof, the number of valleys, dormers, and chimneys, and the chosen material (standard architectural shingles vs. premium designer shingles or flat SBS membranes). Additional factors include the condition of underlying plywood decking and accessibility of the property for disposal bins.
Most standard residential roof replacements in the Greater Toronto Area (1,500 to 2,800 sq ft) are completed within 1 to 2 consecutive days, weather permitting. Complex multi-slope properties or commercial flat roofing installations can require 3 to 5 days. Crews seal all exposed sections at the end of every workday to ensure continuous weather protection.

In many cases, initial digital satellite measurements can provide a preliminary estimate range, which is confirmed with a quick on-site physical inspection to assess ventilation, flashing details, and roof deck condition.
For the Canadian climate, prioritize materials with high wind ratings (rated for lake-effect windstorms), proven cold-weather impact resistance, and Class A fire resistance. In Southern Ontario, where winter freeze-thaw cycles cause ice damming, ensure your roofing specification includes continuous ice & water shield at the eaves and valleys, paired with proper balanced attic intake and exhaust ventilation.
